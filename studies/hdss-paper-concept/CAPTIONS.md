# Proposed figures and tables

These are concept-stage figures generated from an existing technical benchmark. All target numbers refer to synthetic objects. Results must be frozen again after the remaining numerical and planning controls. Main figures are supplied as PNG previews and vector PDF/SVG; tables are editable CSV files. No local clinical cohort is included.

**Figure 1. Benchmark design and separation of effects.** (A) Relative positions of 24 synthetic gross target volumes on one public anatomical background. Colour denotes nominal volume (6.5, 30 or 120 mm³); marker areas aid visibility and are not physical cross-sections. (B) Structure transfer, fixed-dose evaluation and replanning form separate comparisons. Each original plan uses 24 targets and a margin of 0, 1 or 2 mm. The spatial coordinates and original target identities remain paired across routes.

**Figure 2. Target geometry after export and return.** Each point is one observed target representation, compared with its native binary reference. Panels show GTVs and the corresponding 1- and 2-mm PTVs. Dice is computed using binary/contour-slab bodies. This representation differs from the recovered source-grid surface used for the HDSS dose evaluation in Figure 3. Standard export, direct HDSS and four Eclipse import settings each contain 24 targets per panel. Monaco contains 24, 17 and 7 observed targets, respectively. Predicted missing Monaco contours are excluded. The horizontal axis reports actual native volume, not nominal design volume.

**Figure 3. Structure-dependent D98 differences on unchanged dose fields.** Transferred minus original D98 is evaluated with one common complete-volume method on each plan's original fine dose field. Points are individual targets; short bars denote medians; numbers give available target counts. Dashed lines at ±0.5 and ±1.0 Gy are descriptive screening levels, not validated clinical tolerances. HDSS recovery reuses the decoded original source-grid geometry: zero difference is an encoding/reconstruction check, not an independent TPS accuracy result. All dose fields and coordinate conventions are fixed within each pair. Horizontal offsets separate points only.

**Figure 4. Native DVH agreement and geometric preservation are different questions.** (A) Native PTV13 DVHs from the original system and two receiving routes for the 1-mm plan; original RayStation staircase points are preserved. (B) PTV13 evaluated with the common evaluator on the same fine dose field, using original/recovered HDSS, standard-export and Eclipse-return geometries. (C) Native D98 differences versus the original TPS and common fixed-dose differences for all 24 matched PTVs on each route. The dashed diagonal denotes equal differences, not an accuracy reference. PTV13 is starred; it was selected retrospectively for the largest improvement in absolute native D98 agreement of Eclipse relative to RayStation. Differences between panels include the full representation and evaluation routes and do not uniquely identify proprietary DVH algorithms.

**Figure 5. Replanning and the choice of evaluation target.** All four new plans were optimised in the same source planning system, using either returned standard-export targets or targets returned through Eclipse HDSS High, at 1- and 2-mm margins. Each line joins two readings of the same new dose field: on its planning target and on the corresponding original target. Values subtract the original-plan D98 on the original target. There are 24 coupled targets in each of four plans, not 96 independent plan realisations. A zero line indicates the original-plan reference. Unchanged-template repeat controls remain necessary before assigning the entire plan difference to transferred contours.

**Table 1. Synthetic target design.** Nominal volume groups and counts by shape. The four smallest targets are spheres only; the two larger groups each include four spheres, four ellipsoids and two irregular bodies. Spheres and ellipsoids span 0°/30° source-grid orientation and 0/0.5-mm CT phase; irregular bodies use the oblique grid only. Actual stored volumes, rather than analytic nominal volumes, define the native reference. This reduced panel does not support a fully factorial separation of size, shape, orientation and location effects.

**Table 2. Paired fixed-dose summary by route and plan.** For each route, the table reports the number of observed targets, median contour-body Dice, median and range of signed D98 changes, and counts meeting absolute differences of at least 0.5 or 1.0 Gy. The proposed main-paper table uses the 1-mm plan; all three plans are provided in the accompanying CSV. Denominators are the observed target counts. Monaco estimates from generated contours are not included. Contour-body Dice and recovered-surface DVH are separate representation checks, as defined in Figures 2 and 3. Repeated targets and shared plan fields are not independent patient observations.

**Figure S1. Transfer from the MR-linked state to the planning CT.** Twenty-four synthetic GTV geometries are compared before and after transfer with the same registrations. Each panel evaluates those same GTVs on a different original plan dose (0-, 1- or 2-mm planning margin). The panels do not depict three independent sets of target shapes or a registration-accuracy study. Four passive CT controls were unchanged. Numbers in the table use the current complete-volume evaluator.

**Figure S2. Anatomy-free analytical dose-sampling control.** The identical continuous sphere and linear dose field D(x)=20+x Gy (x in mm) are evaluated using interpolated linear dose or values assigned from a 1-mm dose lattice. Displayed centres have a 0.5-mm lattice phase. Both numerical methods use the same volume samples, so the display isolates dose sampling rather than a contour change. Table S1 also includes centres at zero phase and 0.08/0.04-mm integration steps. Exact spherical-cap quantiles provide the analytical D98 reference. This constructed example is not assigned to any commercial TPS.
